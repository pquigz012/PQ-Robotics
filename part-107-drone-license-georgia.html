What's on the test

Everything the FAA tests, in plain English

The Part 107 exam draws from five areas. We teach each one with the real charts and weather reports you'll see on test day, then drill the questions people miss most.

  1. Regulations

    The Part 107 rules: altitude, visual line of sight, flying over people, at night, Remote ID, registration and reporting accidents.

  2. Airspace & charts

    Reading a sectional chart, controlled airspace around Atlanta's airports, LAANC approvals, TFRs and NOTAMs.

  3. Weather

    Decoding METARs and TAFs, density altitude, wind and how weather changes what your drone can do.

  4. Loading & performance

    Weight, balance, center of gravity and load factor, explained by someone who builds drones from scratch.

  5. Operations

    Preflight, crew roles, emergency procedures, radio calls, decision making and the physiology questions.

Getting licensed in Georgia

From class to certificate in five steps

Georgia uses the federal FAA certificate. There is no separate state drone license.

  1. Take the prep class

    In person or online, then keep taking practice tests until you score well above 70%.

  2. Create an IACRA account

    Get your FAA Tracking Number (FTN) online. We walk you through it in class.

  3. Book the exam

    Schedule the knowledge test with PSI at a testing center near you. Bring a government photo ID.

  4. Pass and apply

    Submit your application in IACRA. After a TSA background check, the FAA emails a temporary certificate.

  5. Fly for business

    Your plastic card arrives by mail. Stay current with free FAA online training every 24 months.

Common questions

About the Part 107 class

Do I need a Part 107 license to fly a drone in Georgia?

You need one to fly for any business or paid purpose, anywhere in Georgia. Flying only for fun needs the free FAA TRUST test and registration for drones over 250 grams instead. Georgia has no separate state drone license.

How much does it cost to get a drone license in Georgia?

Our prep class is $399 in person or $199 live online at founding prices, plus the FAA knowledge test fee of about $175, paid to the testing center. The certificate itself is free from the FAA.

How old do you have to be?

You must be at least 16 to hold a Remote Pilot Certificate. Younger students are welcome in class to start learning the material.

Is the exam included in the class?

No. The FAA knowledge test is taken separately at a PSI testing center, which you book and pay for yourself. We show you how to book it in class.

What if I don't pass?

In-person students can sit in on another class free. Every student gets 30 days of questions by text, and the FAA lets you retest after a short waiting period.

Should I take it in person or online?

In person is best if you like to ask questions as you go and work the charts by hand. Online is best if you're outside metro Atlanta or can't give up a Saturday. Both cover the same material.

Do I need to own a drone?

No. The Part 107 exam is a written test. If you also want to learn to fly, the License + First Flight bundle adds a private lesson on our drones or yours.
